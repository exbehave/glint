# BGC: Heartbreakers — the Bad Girls

The working sheet for who's in the game, how each girl plays, and the rules behind her play. Kennedy fills in the blanks; Claude builds from it. Quotes live in the Google Sheet "BGC: Heartbreakers Roster & Quotes".

## How a girl is put together

- **Bio:** one or two cheeky, unflattering lines on her character card, plus a hint at how she plays.
- **Stats card:** a set of dials, each 0–100%. The card *is* her play style: the same card format describes a human's habits on their own stats card. Dials replace the old on/off traits, so opposites (always ducks vs saves her low cards) are just two ends of one dial, and nothing can contradict.
- **How a dial is used:** every time she makes a decision, the game checks the dials that matter for it and rolls a number from 1 to 100 for each. A 10% Moon pass dial means about 1 pass in 10 she hands over her top cards uncovered; the other 9 she covers. Roll once per decision (one roll for the whole pass, not one per card). When several dials apply to one decision they're checked in a fixed order, written per decision when it's built.
- **Judgment** is one dial that sits over all the others: high Judgment means she only follows a habit when it won't cost her (Smart); low Judgment means she follows it even when it backfires (Stubborn).
- **Level:** Easy / Medium / Hard / Genius. Set by her dials, then checked by running thousands of test games and measuring her actual win rate.

**Special abilities** (on or off, Genius girls only, and only a few of them):
- **Learns the table:** watches how everyone plays during the game, the human most of all, and adjusts: stops covering a girl she's caught bluffing, bluffs the girl who always covers, leads into whoever always ducks. Keeps a tally of each player's passes, so someone who keeps passing uncovered moon hands gets read. Her notes carry across games in story mode.
- **Intuit:** sees every stats card at the table before a card is played: the other girls' and each signed-in human's long-term card. Fair play: she never sees anyone's cards.
- **Psychic (she cheats):** actually sees cards she shouldn't. The one deliberate exception to the no-cheating rule; Natalie and Delilah have it, and both see only the human players' hands (for now).

**Pickers** (one choice from a few; Claude's suggestions):
- **Out for you, with more than one human:** Random (default) / Whoever's winning / Whoever beat her last time.
- **Psychic mode:** Sometimes / The humans' hands only / Key moments only / Boss match only. Natalie and Delilah: the humans' hands only, for now.
- **Discard plan, when she's out of the suit led:** Danger first (Q♠, then the A♠/K♠ while the queen is out, then high hearts, then other high cards) / Make more voids (throws from her shortest suit to run out of it too) / Keep my exits (throws high but keeps one low card per suit) / Whatever's highest. Applies on any trick, including the first (where no points can be thrown, so she sheds a high non-point card).
- **Moon payoff:** Smart (default; picks the better of +26 and −26) / Show-off (always everyone else +26) / Safe (always me −26). Easy girls pick at random.

## Priority: which dial wins when two disagree (draft, Kennedy to confirm)

On every decision, dials are checked top to bottom. Each one that applies rolls; **the first dial that fires decides the move**, and everything below it is skipped for that decision. Then Judgment gets the last word.

When she plays a card:
1. **The rules.** Only legal cards count. Always first.
2. **Mood.** Hothead, Gets cocky and Comeback don't pick a card; they raise or lower her Judgment and memory for a while, so they're applied before anything else.
3. **What she knows.** Card memory, Queen tracking, Reads the pass, Learns the table, Intuit and Psychic don't pick a card either; they decide what information the rest of the list works from.
4. **Filters.** Softie and Season sisters remove choices (she won't dump on those players at all) before anything ranks them.
5. **Moon emergencies.** Her own moon attempt in progress (Goes for the moon), or stopping a real one (Covers, Bluffs).
6. **The queen.** Hot potato, Out for you, Keeps the queen.
7. **Who gets the points.** Out for you, Holds a grudge, Takes down the leader, Piles on the loser, in that order.
8. **How she plays the trick.** Trick hog, Ducking, Sheds high cards and her Discard plan.
9. **Judgment's veto.** The cost check compares the chosen card with her best card. If it costs more than her Judgment allows, she plays her best card instead.

Passing has its own shorter list: Keeps the queen, Moon pass, Spade panic, Voids a suit, then Helps the table and Takes down the leader (who she passes her worst to), then Judgment's veto.

**Goal (settles conflicts):** each girl has a ranked goal, and it's the formula the cost check scores every card with. It's what decides when two dials pull different ways. Natalie's goal (Kennedy, Nov 4): **first keep her own points as low as possible, then give the human as many as possible**, using every tool she has. In practice: she finds the cards that cost her the least; among those that are nearly as good (within about half a point), she picks the one that hurts the human most. So if someone else is shooting the moon, she stops it, because a moon would cost her 26. **You first, then me** (Kennedy, Nov 4: Delilah): the same tools as Natalie with the order flipped. She picks the cards that hurt the human most, and only among those does she protect herself, even if it risks her own points. Exploitable: she'll let another girl shoot the moon if it also lands 26 on the human. Other goals to choose from (Claude's suggestions): Me first (just minimize her points), Me first then the leader, Win the game (her total vs the leader's, not just this hand), Chaos (random).

**Strategic dials (Kennedy, Nov 4, from Natalie's moon pass):** for a high-Judgment girl, a dial like Moon pass isn't a careless habit, it's a weapon. Passing her high cards gets danger out of her own hand, so she does it when it helps her, at a mixed rate (about 2 in 5) so a player who's watching can't count on her either way: pass a moon hand, then cover twice, then sometimes cover with a low heart to throw them off. She also reads the receiver: against someone whose card says they rarely try for the moon (low Goes for the moon, heavy Ducking), the risk is near zero, so she passes her high cards every time. This needs Intuit or Learns the table to know the receiver.

**Signature dial:** each girl can still have one dial marked as her signature, which jumps to the top of its own step. Her goal still has the final say.

## How she judges what's good for her (the cost check)

- **Imagined deals:** before a move she deals the cards she can't see many different ways, but only ways that fit what she knows (who's out of a suit, what was passed). For each card she could play, she plays the rest of the hand out in her head and counts the points. The average is that card's cost. This is how Genius plays today; it's the standard method for hidden-card games (the one bridge programs use), not a Stockfish-style full search, since the other hands are hidden.
- **Judgment as a tolerance:** her habits suggest a card; the cost check compares it with her best card. Judgment sets how many extra points she'll accept to follow the habit: 100% accepts none, 0% accepts any. Example: holding the Q♠ for the human gets riskier every trick as her spades run low; the trick it costs more than her Judgment allows, she dumps it.
- **Her goal is the formula** the cost check scores with (see Goal above), so a girl like Natalie compares two numbers per card (her points, then the human's).
- **Memory dials shape the imagined deals:** low Card memory means she imagines deals with cards already played, so her estimates get sloppy; high Queen tracking puts the queen where it most likely is. So a high-Judgment girl can still misjudge, if her information is bad.
- **How accurate (quick test, Nov 4, 12 hands per point, rough):** with 60 imagined deals per card, early in the hand (trick 2) she picks the truly best card 7 times in 12 and one within half a point of it 10 in 12, each card's cost uncertain by about ±0.8 points, in about 21 ms per decision; mid-hand (trick 7) 9 in 12 exact and 12 in 12 within half a point, ±0.3 points, 9 ms. Early misses are mostly near-ties. Four times the samples halves the uncertainty. In the last few tricks there are few enough possible deals to check every one, which would make a Genius endgame nearly perfect (option, not built). Possible deals of the unseen cards: about 84 quadrillion at trick 1, 400 million at trick 7, 1,680 at trick 11.
- **Its known blind spots:** it assumes everyone else plays like the Hard level (Learns the table, Intuit and Psychic fix that), it undervalues keeping her options hidden, and it can't plan a bluff on its own.

## Hand tags

Every hand is read three ways. Low cards = 2–6, middle = 7–10, high = jack–ace. Cutoffs are first guesses; adjust freely.

**Shape tags** (any number can be true at once)

| Tag | Cutoff | How often (200,000 random hands) |
|---|---|---|
| Lots of low | 5+ low cards (probably raise to 6+; 5 of 13 ranks count as low) | 62% |
| Lots of middle | 5+ middle cards | 36% |
| Lots of high | 5+ high cards | 36% |
| Good mix | 3+ low and 3+ high | 81% |
| Heartless | no hearts | not measured yet |
| Loaded | 2+ aces | not measured yet |
| Hard to stop | every heart she holds is a top heart (no one else holds a higher one), or she has none, plus enough high cards to win the tricks that matter | not measured yet |
| One stopper away | exactly one of her hearts can be beaten (a lone K♥) | not measured yet |
| Spoiler heart | lots of high cards but a heart from 2 to 6 that ruins any moon (may narrow; it's in a quarter of hands) | 25% |

Shooting the moon only needs every trick with a heart or the Q♠ in it, so low clubs and diamonds don't spoil a moon. Low hearts do.

**Spade situation** (exactly one)

| Situation | Meaning | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Spades fine | no queen, no exposed A♠ or K♠ | 57% |
| Unprotected queen | the Q♠ with two or fewer low spades | 21% |
| Exposed A♠ or K♠ | high spades without the queen and with two or fewer low spades | 18% |
| Protected queen | the Q♠ with four or more low spades | 4% |

**Short suit:** two or fewer clubs or diamonds (55% of hands).

**Passing changes the hand.** The tags are read twice: before passing (to choose the three cards, aiming at the hand she wants to end up with, and depending on who receives them and which way the pass goes) and again after receiving (her real hand for the round). A pass can turn a good hand bad or a bad hand good, so the test games report both readings: the hand she was dealt and the hand she played.

## Player stats card (planned)

Every signed-in player's games are tracked over time with the same dials as the girls' cards above, so the game can say how someone plays: "You pass an uncovered moon hand 40% of the time", "You duck every time", "You lead spades into the leader".

- **The card:** the player's tendencies, shown on their profile.
- **Coaching:** short tips with the reason behind them, for habits that cost points (not covering your pass hands the receiver a moon; leading spades into the low player lets them dump the queen safely) and for good habits worth doing more.
- **Girls' cards are a mystery at first** (Kennedy, Nov 4). A player can't see a girl's stats card until they've beaten that girl's level; then every girl in that level unlocks. Before that, the bio is the only hint.
- **Intuit girls** read this history before the game starts. Learns the table builds it during the game and, in story mode, across games.
- **Private.** Only the player sees their own card. They can choose to share it (a share button), but no one else can look it up.
- **Every Bad Girl has a stats card too, in the same format** (Kennedy, Nov 4). Her card is what decides how she plays: the game reads her tendencies the same way it would describe a human's.
- **Intuit girls see every stats card at the table:** the other girls' and the humans', even though humans can't see each other's.

## Rules decided so far

- **The queen trumps other reactions.** If the Q♠ is in a trick, only the queen lines play (the girl who dumped it, then the girl stuck with it). The "breaks hearts" quote waits for the end of the trick for that reason. (Built.)
- **End of the game:** the moon line plays, then the win line, even when they're two different girls. (Built.)
- **Quote pile-ups:** when several girls' quotes go off at once, one girl (two at most) is picked at random to speak, rotating so everyone gets a turn before anyone repeats. (Not built yet.)
- **Shooting the moon:** the shooter always chooses "everyone else +26" or "me −26". Scores can go below 0. (Built.)
- **No cheating.** Computer players only use what a real player at the table could know: their own hand, cards played, what was passed to them, and how people have played. They never peek at anyone's hand, except a girl with the Psychic ability, who cheats on purpose.
- **Quotes:** up to three per moment; the game picks one at random. A moment with no quote stays quiet.

## Test plan (the simulator plays every seat)

1. **Fairness check:** four copies of the same girl at one table should each win about 25% of games. Anything else means the table itself is lopsided (seat order, who leads first), and it gets fixed before anything else is measured.
2. **The reference girl:** a fixed, ordinary Medium card that never changes, used as the yardstick. Each real girl plays against three copies of her. About 25% wins means Medium; first-guess bands: Easy under 15%, Hard 35–40%, Genius 45% or more (to be tuned once real numbers come in).
3. **Same deals for everyone (Kennedy's control-group method):** a saved set of deals, each card pre-assigned to a seat. First the baseline: four reference girls play every deal. Then one seat's girl is swapped out and the exact same deals are replayed; the deal-by-deal difference is her effect. She's rotated through all four seats so no seat's advantage counts for or against her, and the random rolls are fixed too (same seed), so the swapped girl is the only thing that changed. Luck cancels out, so far fewer games are needed.
4. **One dial at a time:** change one dial, rerun on the same deals, and see how much it moves her results. That shows which dials matter and how much a habit really costs.
5. **Round robin:** every girl plays mixed tables with every other girl, giving one overall ranking, so the levels line up across the whole roster.
6. **Human stand-ins:** reference cards for a beginner, an average player and a sharp player, to see how each girl treats a human (Out for you, Psychic).

7. **Calibrating the live-moon rule:** the computer girls' logged intent is the answer key. Every candidate rule gets scored against it: "more than one point trick" (the current rule), and "at least N points" for N from 1 to 25, alone or combined. For each one, two numbers: how many of the attempts it flags were real (not accidents), and how many real attempts it catches. Plotted against N, the best rule is where both stay high, plus how early in the hand it catches them. The winner replaces the current rule. Needs girls that really try for the moon (the Goes for the moon dial) first.

Results are reported as win rate and average points per hand (points are steadier than wins, so they settle faster). With 1,000 games a 25% win rate is accurate to about ±3 percentage points.

## Results log (planned; starts once the engine is settled)

The test runner keeps a running record for every girl, in a results file next to this one, and every change to a girl's card or to the engine gets an entry showing her numbers before and after.

**Per girl:**
- Win rate against the reference girl and in the round robin, and her overall ranking.
- Average points per hand (steadier than wins), and how often she ends a hand with 0.
- How often she catches the Q♠, and how often she lands it on someone else (on a human, for Out for you girls).
- Moon attempts, moons made, and moons she stopped, counted two ways:
  - **What she meant (computer players only):** the runner logs her decisions, so it knows exactly when her Goes for the moon dial switched her into moon mode, and when Covers made her take a heart to stop someone. That's her real intent, not a guess.
  - **What anyone could see (works for humans too, and for the stats card):** a **live moon** is when one player has won more than one trick with points in it and nobody else has taken any points (Kennedy, Nov 4: no point minimum). An *observed attempt* = she had a live moon going. A *stop* = she takes a point card while someone else has a live moon. A point trick taken before anyone has a live moon is not a stop, since no moon was on yet.
- How often each stand-out dial fired, and what it cost or saved her on average.
- Results by hand tag (does she fall apart on All middle hands?) and before vs after the pass.

**Change log:** date, what changed (one dial, a goal, the engine), and the before/after numbers for every girl it touched.

**Versions:** each engine change starts a new version. Numbers are kept per version, so an old result never gets mixed with a new one; "all-time" totals are only compared within the same version.

### Built: the first four girls (Nov 5, 2026, engine v1)

Their cards live in `girls.js` (`GIRLS`); the game and the test runner both use it. How each dial is coded:
- **Judgment's veto:** when a dial picks a different card from her base brain's card, she imagines 24 deals and compares the two. If the habit costs more than her tolerance, she plays the base card. Tolerance = 3 × (1 − J) ÷ J points: Judi (70%) ≈ 1.3, the Twins (40%) ≈ 4.5, Amy (10%) ≈ 27, which is more than a whole hand, so Amy is never overruled.
- **Hot potato:** whenever the Q♠ is playable and can't win the trick, roll: dump it.
- **Holds a grudge:** after each trick she wins, whoever put the Q♠ on it (or the off-suit heart dumper on her biggest heart trick so far) becomes her target for the rest of the game. When she's out of the suit led: if the target is winning the trick, she loads it with the Q♠ or her highest heart; if not, she keeps the queen for later.
- **Takes down the leader:** the same, aimed at the other player with the lowest game total.
- **Breaks hearts early:** while hearts are unbroken and she could play one, roll: she does (her highest on a discard, her lowest on a lead). A miss means she holds them: if her base brain was about to break hearts, she plays a non-heart instead. So Judi (5%) almost never breaks hearts and Amy (60%) usually does.
- **Ducking:** following suit, roll: slip under the winning card with her highest card that still loses. (Hard already does this most of the time, so on Judi it changes little.)
- **Hothead:** after a trick worth 5+ points, roll: for 3 tricks she slams her highest card.
